# Reflection of the open boundary under a synthetic long-wave pulse

*Run 30 September 2026 on D-Flow FM 2026.01 (dflowfm-cli, serial, Windows). Cases built by `scripts/build_riemann_pulse_test.py`, analysed by `scripts/analyse_riemann_pulse_test.py`. Figure at `figures/riemann_pulse_test.png`. The model inputs are regenerated by the build script and are not versioned.*

The model plan requires a weakly reflective open boundary, on the premise that a prescribed water level reflects outgoing long waves into the domain and contaminates the seiche signal, and it requires the Riemann boundary of FM to be tested before any production run. The test below measures both boundary types against a reference without a boundary.

---

## 1. Design

A Gaussian hump of water level, 0.2 m high, is released from rest on a flat bed at 150 m, the depth of the Malta Plateau where the boundary of domain B will lie, on a uniform mesh of 1500 m, the offshore cell size proposed there. Each configuration is run three times, with a Riemann boundary forced by a zero incoming signal, with a water level prescribed at zero, and in a reference domain large enough that no reflection reaches a station within the window. Inside the common area the reference differs from the test only by the absence of the boundary, so the difference between the two records at a station is the reflected wave, and incident and reflected waves need not be separated in time.

| Configuration | Geometry | Pulse time scale | Stations | Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Channel | 3 cells wide, boundary 200 km from the hump | 5 and 20 min | 100 km, 180 km, boundary cell | 4 h |
| Basin, 120 km | square, boundary on all sides | 5 min | boundary cells at 0, 22.5, 30 and 45°, mid-radius | 2.5 h |
| Basin, 240 km | square, boundary on all sides | 5 min | as above | 3 h |

Coriolis is disabled so that the boundary is tested in isolation. The Riemann formulation of FM prescribes the incoming invariant in terms of the normal velocity alone and linearises about a fluid initially at rest with no residual current (Technical Reference Manual, section 6.4.2.2). It is therefore exact for a plane wave at normal incidence in the linear limit, and for oblique incidence first-order theory gives a reflection coefficient (1 − cos θ)/(1 + cos θ).

The peak reflection coefficient is max|test − reference| / max|reference|, signed by the reflected wave at its extremum. The spectral coefficient is the ratio of the amplitude spectra of the reflected and incident records, reported where the incident spectrum holds at least a hundredth of its peak energy and the record spans at least one cycle.

## 2. Normal incidence

| Pulse | Station | Riemann, peak | Water level, peak | Riemann, 0.5 cph | 1 cph | 2 cph | 4 cph |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 min | 100 km | −0.013 | −0.941 | 0.006 | 0.011 | 0.023 | 0.046 |
| 5 min | 180 km | +0.014 | −0.972 | 0.006 | 0.011 | 0.024 | |
| 5 min | boundary cell | −0.012 | −0.990 | 0.006 | 0.011 | 0.022 | |
| 20 min | 100 km | −0.003 | −0.995 | 0.005 | | | |
| 20 min | 180 km | −0.003 | −0.998 | 0.006 | 0.011 | | |
| 20 min | boundary cell | −0.003 | −0.999 | 0.006 | 0.011 | | |

The prescribed water level returns the incident pulse inverted and almost undiminished, with coefficients between −0.94 and −1.00, which confirms the premise of the model plan at the depth and resolution of domain B. The Riemann boundary reflects 1.3 per cent of the 5-minute pulse and 0.3 per cent of the 20-minute pulse. The spectral coefficient rises from 0.6 per cent at 0.5 cph to 2.3 per cent at 2 cph and 4.6 per cent at 4 cph, a growth with the number of cells per wavelength that is consistent with discretisation error rather than with the formulation. Across the milgħuba band of 0.2 to 2 cph the reflection at normal incidence remains between one half and two and a half per cent of the incident amplitude.

## 3. Oblique incidence

| Incidence angle | First-order theory | Riemann, 120 km basin | Riemann, 240 km basin | Water level, 120 km | Water level, 240 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0° | 0.000 | +0.076 | +0.049 | −0.961 | −0.972 |
| 22.5° | 0.040 | +0.066 | +0.048 | −0.964 | −0.976 |
| 30° | 0.072 | +0.088 | +0.065 | −0.971 | −0.978 |
| 45°, corner cell | 0.172 | +0.288 | −0.239 | −1.033 | −1.033 |

Up to 30 degrees the Riemann boundary reflects between 5 and 9 per cent of the incident amplitude, which exceeds first-order theory at small angles and approaches it at 30 degrees. The excess at normal incidence falls by a third when the distance from the source to the boundary doubles, which attributes part of it to the curvature of the cylindrical front, since the condition assumes a plane wave. A floor of some 3 to 5 per cent remains. The corner cells, where two boundaries meet, reflect 24 to 29 per cent against 17 per cent in theory.

The mean level over the 120 km basin, as a fraction of its initial value, departs from the reference under the Riemann boundary by 0.17 at one hour, 0.09 at 1.5 hours and 0.02 from two hours onward, and is +0.002 at 2.5 hours against −0.020 in the reference. Under the prescribed level it oscillates between −1.9 and +2.1 over the same window without decay, since the pulse is held in the basin as a trapped mode.

## 4. Consequences for the configuration

**The Riemann boundary is adopted for domain B.** At normal incidence it reflects less than 2.5 per cent in the band of interest, and a prescribed level reflects all of it.

The reflected energy is the square of these coefficients. At 5 to 9 per cent in amplitude it is below one per cent of the incident energy for incidence up to 30 degrees. A wave radiated by the harbours and reflected at a boundary 20 to 60 km away, the southern side of domain B being the nearest, returns after 20 to 50 minutes on the plateau, attenuated by geometric spreading over the round trip in addition to the reflection coefficient.

Three configuration choices follow.

1. **Corners are kept distant from the area of interest.** Their reflection is three to four times that of a straight boundary. Domain B places them on the open plateau, at more than 40 km from the harbours.
2. **A boundary spanning several sides is written as one polyline.** FM 2026.01 reads only the first polyline of a `.pli` file and ignores the others with a warning alone. The unmatched sides become closed walls, and a first build of this test returned reflection coefficients near +1 on that account. The analysis script checks the number of cells opened against the expected count and stops on a mismatch.
3. **The boundary is kept normal to the principal direction of propagation where the geometry allows.** Reflection rises steeply beyond 30 degrees.

---

## 5. Cases outside the present test

The test establishes the behaviour of the boundary under the conditions its formulation assumes. Four departures from them occur in production and are to be tested before the first production run.

- **Residual current.** The formulation linearises about a fluid at rest. A boundary crossed by the mean circulation of the Malta Channel, forced by CMEMS, may treat the residual as an outgoing perturbation.
- **Non-zero incoming signal.** The tide and the low-frequency sea level from CMEMS enter through the incoming invariant. Whether the boundary transmits that signal while still absorbing the outgoing seiche is the property production requires.
- **Variable depth along the boundary.** The boundary of domain B crosses depths between 100 and 190 m.
- **Coriolis.** Over the plateau the Rossby radius of the barotropic mode is some 450 km, larger than the domain, so the effect is expected to be small, but it is untested.
