# Sea level spectrum inside the Grand Harbour from the Senglea gauge

*Analysed 1 October 2026. Downloaded by `scripts/download_jrc_tad_wl.py`, cleared of radar echoes by `scripts/despike_tad_5s.py`, analysed by `scripts/analyse_senglea_spectrum.py`, figures by `scripts/figure_senglea_record.py`. Figures `figures/senglea_spectrum.png`, `senglea_spectrogram.png`, `senglea_record_overview.png` and `senglea_events.png`. The data are not versioned and are regenerated by the download script.*

The basin mode calculation of [basin_modes.md](basin_modes.md) places the fundamental mode of the Grand Harbour between 14.5 and 18.4 minutes, 3.3 to 4.1 cph, above the milgħuba band of 0.2 to 2 cph. A sea level record inside the basin tests that prediction directly.

## 2. Quality control

**Radar echoes.** Anything entering the beam above the water, the hull or superstructure of a vessel, heavy rain or spray, shortens the measured range and appears as a rise of the level. At Senglea these echoes arrive in bursts of several minutes, are always upward and saturate near −0.02 m. In dense bursts they dominate a running median. Since the contamination is one-sided, the water surface is taken as the lower envelope, the 20th percentile of the 5 s samples over one minute, and samples more than 0.08 m above it are discarded. A minute in which more than a quarter of the samples are discarded is set missing. The procedure removes 7.6 per cent of the samples at Senglea and 0.45 per cent at Marsaxlokk, and a clean oscillation of 0.35 m range passes unaltered.

**Reference offsets.** The Senglea record contains episodes of hours during which the level is displaced by 1.3 to 2.1 m, entered and left within one or two minutes, during which the basin oscillation either persists on top of the offset or disappears. These are removed where a step up and a step down of matching size bound them, and wherever the hourly median departs from the three-day median by more than 0.6 m. The tide at Malta reaches some 0.15 m and a surge departs from a three-day median by much less, so the criterion does not reach a physical signal.

Stuck values, isolated jumps above 0.3 m in a minute and residual spikes are removed as well, and gaps up to five minutes are interpolated.

**Windows admitted to the analysis.** Two windows of the Senglea record are retained, **24 June to 31 December 2021** and **1 December 2022 to 31 March 2023**, some nine months in all. They are chosen on two criteria measured on the despiked series.

| Period | Monthly coverage | Daily median level |
|---|---|---|
| 24 Jun to 31 Dec 2021, window A | 84 to 100 % | −0.53 to −0.60 m |
| Jan to Nov 2022 | 0 to 58 % | variable, +1.59 m in July |
| 1 Dec 2022 to 31 Mar 2023, window B | 66 to 96 % | −0.54 to −0.83 m |
| Apr 2023 to Sep 2024 | 3 to 34 % | +1.1 to +1.8 m |

Outside the windows the coverage falls to 58 per cent or less, and from April 2023 the level sits near +1.5 m, a shift of some 2.1 m indicating a change in the installation or configuration of the radar. The first three days after installation are excluded for level steps and plateaus held for hours. After quality control window A is 94.6 per cent valid and window B 87.6 per cent.

**The choice of windows is to be confirmed with Prof. Gauci**, together with the cause of the gaps of 2022, the cause of the shift of 2023, and whether the gauge was moved, since a different position would alter the local expression of the basin modes and would separate window B from window A. The absolute level is not used in this analysis, and the shift is therefore not corrected.

## 3. Mean spectrum

Welch's method over 207 gap-free segments of 2048 minutes, Hann window, resolution 0.029 cph. Peaks are reported where the spectrum stands at least twice above a background taken as the running median of the log spectrum over a factor of two in frequency.

| Frequency | Period | Peak over background | Relation to the prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.61 cph | 23.0 min | 23.8 | outside the predicted band, longer |
| 3.02 cph | 19.9 min | 2.5 | |
| 3.57 cph | 16.8 min | 10.1 | within the predicted band, 14.5 to 18.4 min |
| 5.98 cph | 10.0 min | 7.6 | |
| 8.67 cph | 6.9 min | 15.2 | |

The spectrogram shows all four principal peaks on practically every day of both windows, in 2021 and in 2022–23 alike, independently of the forcing. They are therefore properties of the geometry. The peak at 6.9 minutes falls within the range of 4 to 6 minutes estimated for the inlets and probably belongs to Dockyard Creek or French Creek, on either side of the gauge.

Within the 0.2 to 10 cph band, 12 per cent of the variance of the mean spectrum lies in the milgħuba band.

## 4. Ratio against the gauge outside the harbour

Amplitude ratio, the square root of the ratio of power spectra, over 157 segments common to Senglea and Marsaxlokk in window A.

| Band | Senglea / Marsaxlokk |
|---|---|
| 0.2 to 0.5 cph | 0.87 |
| 0.5 to 1 cph | 1.33 |
| 1 to 2 cph | 1.72 |
| 2 to 3 cph | 2.79 |
| 3.26 to 4.14 cph | 0.54 |
| 5 to 8 cph | 0.98 |

At the lowest frequencies of the band the two sites oscillate with nearly the same amplitude, and the ratio rises toward the upper edge of the band. That pattern agrees with the quasi-static response computed in the sizing exercise, where the gain of the basin rises from 1.0 at 0.2 cph to 1.5 at 2 cph. Between 2 and 3 cph the ratio reaches 2.8, and some 7 at the peak itself, so the 23 minute oscillation is generated or amplified locally rather than arriving with equal amplitude along the coast. Marsaxlokk lies in an embayment with modes of its own, and the ratio below one at 3.3 to 4.1 cph reflects a mode there. The ratio compares two coastal sites and is not a transfer function from the open sea.

## 5. Events

The ten days of largest range in the 0.2 to 10 cph band, among days at least 90 per cent covered and with a gap-free window of 12 hours around the maximum, are all oscillations of the 23 minute mode, with a range of 0.26 to 0.35 m and between 2 and 14 per cent of the variance in the milgħuba band. The largest is 8 November 2021, with a range of 0.35 m. The days of 7 and 8 February 2023, with daily ranges of 0.32 and 0.37 m, fall during the easterly storm of February 2023 recorded by Mazas and Farrugia and are excluded from the ranking only for gaps in the window. No event dominated by the milgħuba band, of size comparable to the basin mode, occurs in the nine months admitted.

## 6. Reading against the prediction

**The basin modes lie outside the milgħuba band, as predicted.** Every peak above the background is at 2.6 cph or higher, a period of 23 minutes or shorter, against the 30 minutes at the upper edge of the band. The margin is narrower than the factor of 1.6 of the basin mode calculation, a factor of 1.3 on the 23 minute peak.

**The dominant peak is longer than the predicted fundamental.** Two readings are compatible with a single gauge.

1. The 23 minute peak is the fundamental mode of the Grand Harbour and the one-dimensional calculation underestimates it by about a quarter. The calculation omits the two-dimensional contraction at the breakwater, represents the branches as oscillating in phase, and borrows its mouth correction from an open rectangular basin, and each of these lengthens a real mode.
2. The 16.8 minute peak, inside the predicted band, is the fundamental of the Grand Harbour, and the 23 minute peak belongs to a larger system, the outer approach shared by the two harbours or the two harbours coupled through it.

The second reading is consistent with the calculation and the first is consistent with the dominance of the peak inside the basin. The distinction is made by the modal structure, which the model will provide by its response to a broadband impulse, and would be made observationally by a second gauge, in Marsamxett or at Portomaso outside both basins.

**The milgħuba band is present but not dominant in the window.** Its share of the short-period variance is 12 per cent, and the largest oscillations of the nine months are the basin mode. Whether the record contains a milgħuba event of the kind documented at Msida remains to be established against the dates of reported events, which the host group may hold.

## 7. Matters for the host group

1. The choice of windows, the cause of the gaps of 2022 and of the level shift of 2023, and whether the gauge was relocated.
2. Dates of milgħuba events reported between June 2021 and March 2023, against which the record can be searched.
3. Access to the Portomaso record at one-minute resolution or finer, which would give a gauge outside both basins on the same coast and decide between the two readings of Section 6.
