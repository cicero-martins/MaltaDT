# Research question and supporting literature

*Formulation discussed within the Oceanography Malta Research Group, October 2026.*

The study addresses the circulation and the renewal of the water of the Valletta harbours, the Grand Harbour and Marsamxett, with a coupled wave and hydrodynamic model in Delft3D FM and SWAN. The atmospherically generated long wave known locally as the milgħuba is treated as a component of that study, since it floods Msida at the head of Marsamxett and since the oscillation it excites is one of the candidate mechanisms of exchange. Sections 1 and 6 concern that component, and Sections 2 to 5 the study as a whole.

---

## 1. The milgħuba as a meteotsunami

The milgħuba is an atmospherically generated long wave in the tsunami frequency band, a meteotsunami, and it is treated as such throughout, in distinction from a seiche in the general sense of water oscillating in a basin. The Mediterranean instances carry local names, *rissaga* in the Balearics, where the harbour of Ciutadella on Menorca is the canonical case, *šćiga* in the Adriatic, and *marrobbio* on the southwestern coast of Sicily, the last of these occurring on the same shelf system and the adjacent coast.

The meteotsunami literature, consolidated by Monserrat, Vilibić and Rabinovich (2006) and set out for harbours by Rabinovich (2009), both listed in Section 9, supplies a three-stage model of amplification which corresponds to the domain analysis recorded in [domain_and_discretisation.md](domain_and_discretisation.md).

**Stage one, Proudman resonance.** Energy transfers from the atmosphere to the sea most efficiently when an atmospheric pressure disturbance travels at the celerity of the long wave beneath it, U = √(gh). The condition selects the part of the shelf that generates the signal.

| Depth | √(gh) | |
|---|---|---|
| 60 m | 24.3 m/s | 87 km/h |
| 100 m | 31.3 m/s | 113 km/h |
| 150 m | 38.4 m/s | 138 km/h |
| 190 m | 43.2 m/s | 155 km/h |

The Malta Plateau, measured at 99 to 190 m on the north transect and 70 to 137 m on the east, is resonant with disturbances travelling between 94 and 155 km/h, which lies within the range of mesoscale convective systems and atmospheric gravity waves.

**Stage two, shoaling.** Green's law gives an amplification of (h₁/h₂)^¼ on the approach. With 150 m taken as a representative depth of the Malta Plateau, the factor to the Grand Harbour at 16 m is 1.75 and to Marsamxett at 13 m it is 1.84. Over the measured range of the plateau, 70 to 190 m, the factor lies between 1.45 and 1.86 for the Grand Harbour and between 1.52 and 1.96 for Marsamxett. At Ciutadella, where the inlet is 5 m deep, the same starting depth would give 2.34, which is larger than either Valletta figure and smaller than the fourfold shoaling reported there, so the reported value includes more than the depth ratio and its composition remains to be checked against the source.

**Stage three, basin resonance.** An eigenvalue calculation on the measured profiles, reported in [basin_modes.md](basin_modes.md), gives fundamental periods of 14.5 to 18.4 minutes for the Grand Harbour and 10.4 to 12.8 minutes for Marsamxett, both shorter than the 30 minutes at the upper edge of the milgħuba band.

At Ciutadella the literature reports approximately fourfold amplification by shoaling on the approach and a further threefold within the harbour, an order of magnitude in total over the wave generated by Proudman resonance. No equivalent decomposition has been published for Malta.

## 2. Prior work

**Drago, A.F. (1999).** *A study on the sea level variations and the 'Milgħuba' phenomenon in the coastal waters of the Maltese Islands.* Doctoral dissertation, University of Southampton. Establishes the phenomenon from sea level gauges and offshore bottom pressure, attributes it to mesoscale atmospheric gravity waves, and analyses sea level variability across timescales by wavelet methods. The treatment concerns the northern coast and Mellieħa Bay.

**Drago, A.F. (2009).** *Sea level variability and the 'Milgħuba' seiche oscillations in the northern coast of Malta, Central Mediterranean.* Physics and Chemistry of the Earth 34, 948–970. The published treatment, drawing on 43 months of densely sampled sea level at Mellieħa Bay between 1993 and 1996. Establishes the band of 0.2 to 2 cph, the masking of the tidal signal, and the interpretation as shelf-scale resonance amplified within the embayments.

**Numerical modelling of coastal seiches in Malta.** Physics and Chemistry of the Earth, 2007, PII S1474706507000551. Authorship to be confirmed, most probably Drago and co-workers. This is the closest prior work on the phenomenon. It simulates the generation and propagation of seiche oscillations in two adjacent wide-mouthed embayments of the northern coast, Mellieħa Bay and St Paul's Bay, using a two-dimensional depth-averaged version of the Princeton Ocean Model, and examines their response to prescribed offshore long-period waves. It reports that standing oscillations generated cross-shore on the shelf, that is leaky waves, force the extreme oscillations in the embayments more effectively than alongshore-propagating edge waves.

**Mazas, F. and Farrugia, C.** *Protecting Valletta's Grand Harbour against adverse wave conditions.* Coastal Engineering Proceedings, paper 154. ARTELIA and Infrastructure Malta. Copy at `reference/`. Offshore sea states were hindcast over the Mediterranean from January 1992 to June 2019 from satellite wind and wave measurements and the directional climate off Valletta established at NW 45 per cent, NE 19 per cent, E 15 per cent and SE 20 per cent. A wave disturbance model was then built over the harbour in PHAROS, the Deltares harbour agitation code, and a transfer matrix of 36 period and direction combinations interpolated onto eight analysis areas. The work records that the site is deep at approximately 20 m, steeply sloped and bounded by a highly reflective rocky foreshore including cliffs, so that waves entering the harbour undergo multiple reflections with little dissipation. The protection scheme it defines is described in Section 2.1.

**Drago, A.F. (2018).** *Designing an observing and forecasting system for the Maltese Islands.* Progress report, Action A.7 of LIFE 16 IPE MT 008, prepared for the Energy and Water Agency. Copy at `reference/`. The design document of the group for a national observing and forecasting system. It records the modelling infrastructure in operation at that date, namely the ROSARIO forecasting system built on the Princeton Ocean Model at 1/64° and 1/96°, WAM at 1/8°, a SWAN downscaling to the embayments on a regular grid of 1/500°, and a SHYFEM finite-element implementation on an unstructured mesh covering the Maltese Islands and the Malta Channel as far as the southern Sicilian coast. It states the intention to establish local-scale models in proximity to and inside the five principal harbours, lists harbour flushing, siltation and water quality among the process models to be linked to them, names the Deltares suite among the model families to be reviewed for that role, and carries three Deltares staff on its international working group. The programme of the report was not continued. Of the systems it lists, the SWAN downscaling remains in use, together with the HF radar data, and the SHYFEM implementation is not running (A. Gauci, personal communication, 9 October 2026).

### 2.1 The Grand Harbour protection scheme

The scheme defined by ARTELIA for Infrastructure Malta comprises an outer breakwater at the tip of the Sciberras Peninsula to block the most severe waves from the northwest, a detached submerged breakwater along the peninsula to dissipate northeasterly and easterly energy by depth-induced breaking, and two revetments at the foot of the cliffs inside the harbour. Against an operational criterion of 0.3 m the downtime is generally halved. The submerged structure was tested in the wave flume and the outer breakwater in the wave basin at the ARTELIA laboratory, with reflection reduced by between 23 and 74 per cent.

#### Stage of the works in the public record

| | Status |
|---|---|
| **Existing breakwater, 1910** | Built. Two arms, 370 m from Fort St Elmo and 120 m from Fort Ricasoli. Repaired by Infrastructure Malta in a €1 million programme completed around 2020, covering coping stones, deck slabs and the 2012 steel bridge to Valletta. This is restoration of the historic structure and is unrelated to the protection scheme |
| **Protection scheme, ARTELIA** | Not built. Announced in February 2025 at €55 million. Reported as an outer breakwater of some 100 m beyond the existing St Elmo arm and a detached submerged breakwater of some 600 m, with revetments inside the harbour |
| **Grand Harbour Revival Plan** | Public consultation launched February 2026 by the Office of the Prime Minister, a four-phase long-term regeneration of the harbour within which the wave protection sits. Responses closed 19 March 2026 |

The structures are in design and consultation rather than in execution, and the harbour represented in this study is the present one. Whether the scheme has advanced to a planning application or a permit is not established from the public record as consulted on 25 September 2026, and the group is better placed to say.

The structures would alter the geometry of the mouth of a basin whose long-wave behaviour and whose exchange with the sea are governed by that mouth. The instrument used to dimension them, a phase-resolving harbour agitation model driven by a matrix of wind-wave periods and directions, computes neither of those quantities, which is a statement about the scope of that instrument and not a criticism of its application.

#### Present geometry of the mouth

In the coastline dataset used for the model the St Elmo arm of the 1910 breakwater is present as a detached polygon 378 m long and 58 m wide, separated from the shore by 42 m, which is the span carried by the steel bridge and which is correctly open water for a hydrodynamic model. The measured length agrees with the documented 370 m.

The entrance is therefore a two-part opening partly closed by the historic breakwater, the main passage and the 42 m gap under the bridge. The eigenvalue calculation of [basin_modes.md](basin_modes.md), made on the measured profile, finds that the breakwater changes the fundamental period by 0.1 minute, since the section at the entrance is within 6 per cent of the median section of the basin and the inertia of the mode is distributed along the harbour. The effect of the same structures on the exchange of water is a separate quantity and is the object of Section 4.2.

The 120 m Ricasoli arm joins the land at Fort Ricasoli and would appear within the mainland polygon rather than as a detached feature, so its representation is not yet established and is to be verified on the mesh.

### 2.2 Implications for the contribution

The Grand Harbour has been modelled for wind waves and for agitation. Its circulation, its renewal and its response in the long-wave band remain untreated, and no hydrodynamic model at the scale of the harbours is in operation within the group.

Harbour flushing at the five principal harbours appears in the design document of 2018 as a target of the local-scale modelling system, in support of Water Framework Directive and Marine Strategy Framework Directive obligations, with the Deltares suite named among the candidate tools and a relocatable setup for ad hoc local domains among the requirements. That programme was not carried out, and the circulation inside the harbours is the principal interest stated by the group for the present work. The renewal question of Section 4.1 therefore corresponds to an objective that remains open.

The prior work on the milgħuba is two-dimensional and depth-averaged, so the vertical structure and any consequence for the renewal of harbour water are also untreated. The 2007 study establishes the response of a Maltese embayment to an imposed offshore long wave, and it prescribes that wave rather than producing it. The coupling between the atmospheric disturbance and the shelf, and the partition of amplification between the shelf, the approach and the basin, are therefore open for Malta, and the group reports no unpublished work on them.

The ARTELIA work also records that the basin is bounded by reflective rock and masonry and dissipates little of what enters it, which raises the quality factor of any resonance it supports. The forced-oscillator estimate in [domain_and_discretisation.md](domain_and_discretisation.md), computed without damping, is on that account less conservative than the omission of damping would ordinarily make it.

## 3. Formulation of the question

A study of this kind is conventionally posed as a test of whether the coupled framework reproduces the observed sea level and currents of the two harbours. Posed in that way the outcome is binary and unequally informative, since an affirmative result confirms what is expected of a mature solver and a negative one is attributable to the configuration rather than to the physics. The object of knowledge is then the model rather than the harbour system, and the result is of interest chiefly to the modelling community, whereas the work contributes to a national pilot whose users are the port and water authorities.

The questions of Section 4 are selected on the criterion that observation alone cannot answer them. Gauges and a buoy record the behaviour of the sea at a few points, whereas a residence time for each inlet, the attribution of the exchange to mechanism and the evaluation of a geometry that does not exist all require a model.

## 4. Research questions

### 4.1 Principal, renewal and circulation of the harbours

> What renews the water of the Grand Harbour and of Marsamxett, at what rate for each basin and for each inlet, and how is that renewal partitioned between wind, density differences, the exchange with the shelf and the oscillation driven by long waves?

Both harbours are microtidal, the tide at Malta reaching some 0.15 m, and neither receives an appreciable freshwater input, so no single forcing is expected to dominate the exchange. A residence time for each inlet cannot be measured, and its attribution to mechanism requires that each forcing be withheld in turn, which only a model permits. Alternating currents displace water without necessarily exchanging it, which is the classical return-flow problem, so the share of the long-wave oscillation that constitutes renewal rather than reversible displacement is part of the question.

The expected results are a residence time for each basin and for each inlet, the inlets around Manoel Island in Marsamxett being of particular interest to the group, the temperature field and its relation to flushing, and current fields suited to the assessment of oil spills from the industrial area of the Grand Harbour. Both harbours are heavily modified water bodies under the Water Framework Directive, and contamination of the sediments of the Grand Harbour is documented by Romeo et al. (2015), an environmental quality assessment published in Environmental Monitoring and Assessment. The question exercises the three-dimensional and Lagrangian components of the framework.

### 4.2 Counterfactual, geometry of the harbours

> How sensitive are the renewal and the long-wave response of the harbours to their geometry, at the mouth of the Grand Harbour and at the structures built inside the basins?

Three modifications are considered, as indicated by the group. They are the breakwaters of the Grand Harbour as modified in the protection scheme of Section 2.1, a closure of the 42 m gap under the St Elmo bridge, and new piers inside the inlets, of which the one built at Msida is the example.

The question is posed as an academic exercise on the sensitivity of the basins and not as an assessment of any particular design. No part of this work is intended to bear on the project being carried forward by Infrastructure Malta, and it is not framed as a review of that project or of the engineering behind it. The planned structures enter as realistic magnitudes for the perturbation, in place of arbitrary ones. Should the findings prove informative, whether and how they are communicated further is a matter for the group and belongs to a later stage.

A basin can be gauged only in the geometry it has, so the sensitivity of its response to a geometry it does not have is a counterfactual that requires a model, which is the criterion of Section 3. The exercise does not repeat the ARTELIA work, since a phase-resolving agitation model driven by a matrix of wind-wave periods and directions and a three-dimensional circulation model compute different quantities, and the first is the established treatment of harbour agitation, which this study does not attempt.

The exercise depends on Section 4.1, since the renewal of the present geometry is the reference against which each modification is measured. The relation between the cross-section of a harbour mouth, its pumping mode and its renewal is a property of the class of basin rather than of Valletta, and a result expressed as a response curve applies to engineered harbours of comparable aspect.

#### Scoping of the magnitude

`scripts/estimate_entrance_restriction.py` computes the sensitivity of the pumping mode to a restriction of the mouth under the idealisation of the basin as a Helmholtz resonator. In that idealisation the period scales as the inverse square root of the open cross-section, so the shift is independent of basin area and of the length of the connecting channel.

| Restriction of the mouth section | Shift in the pumping period | Grand Harbour period |
|---|---|---|
| none | 0 % | 12.4 to 17.5 min |
| 25 per cent | +15.5 % | 14.3 to 20.2 min |
| 50 per cent | +41.4 % | 17.5 to 24.7 min |
| 66 per cent | +71.5 % | 21.2 to 30.0 min |

The idealisation overstates the shift for the Grand Harbour. An eigenvalue calculation on the measured profile, reported in [basin_modes.md](basin_modes.md), finds the section at the entrance within 6 per cent of the median section of the basin, so the inertia of the mode is distributed along the harbour rather than concentrated at the mouth. A restriction of a quarter over a reach of 100 to 500 m then lengthens the period by 1.5 to 4 per cent rather than 15.5, and a restriction of a half by 4 to 12 per cent rather than 41.4.

Even on the Helmholtz figures, reaching 2 cph would require the mouth to lose between 66 and 83 per cent of its section, which exceeds any restriction the reported dimensions suggest. The finding of [domain_and_discretisation.md](domain_and_discretisation.md) that the harbours respond rather than resonate is therefore not altered by a restriction of that order. A metre of sea level rise displaces the same modes by 3.1 per cent on the same profiles (Section 6.3), so the two perturbations are of the same order and neither dominates the mode.

The effect on exchange is separate from the effect on the mode, and it is the larger of the two. The volume passing the mouth per cycle scales with the open section directly rather than with its square root, and residence time responds accordingly. Exchange is the quantity of principal interest to the group, and it is the principal quantity of the counterfactual.

These figures establish the order of the sensitivity and nothing further. They rest on idealised profiles and on a mouth cross-section that Section 2.1 shows to be simplified, the restriction is swept as a parameter, and no particular design is represented.

### 4.3 Component, the long wave and the attribution of amplification

> Of the amplification between the open sea and the heads of the Valletta harbours, how is it partitioned between Proudman resonance over the Malta Plateau, shoaling on the approach, and resonance of the harbour basins themselves, and which atmospheric disturbance speeds and directions maximise that chain?

The milgħuba causes little damage in the harbours as a whole and is of consequence at Msida, at the head of Msida Creek, where the flooding of Section 6.1 is documented. The question is retained as a contained component, since no arrangement of gauges separates the three stages, since the generation of the wave is open for Malta (Section 2.2), and since the oscillation it excites is one of the mechanisms of exchange of Section 4.1. Its result takes the form of a map of exposure by inlet and by quay.

## 5. Experimental design

The design has four parts, taken in the order of their cost.

**Sweep of the long wave.** A moving atmospheric pressure disturbance of fixed amplitude is imposed over the domain and its speed, direction and width are swept, in place of the hindcast of an individual event. The model then maps the resonance structure of the shelf and harbour system, which is a property of the geometry rather than of any particular storm. The approach is the one taken in the Ciutadella literature. ERA5 does not resolve the atmospheric gravity waves that initiate the milgħuba, and under a sweep it is not required to, since no event is being reproduced.

The working proposal is six speeds bracketing 94 to 155 km/h, eight directions and two widths, that is 96 scenarios of 12 simulated hours each. The runs are two-dimensional and barotropic, the wavelength of the long wave being hundreds of times the depth. At the 611 s of serial computation per simulated day measured on the working mesh, the sweep takes some 8 hours, and it is run first, as the initial test of the configuration. It requires a pressure field varying in space and time in D-Flow FM, a weakly reflective open boundary under that forcing, and a response close to linear in the amplitude of the disturbance, the last of which is to be verified.

The observations then serve as the test rather than as the forcing. The dated events are located on the response surface through the one-minute pressure records of the PORTO network, to examine whether they occur under the conditions the model identifies as resonant, which is a prediction open to refutation.

**Hindcast.** A three-dimensional hindcast of some 40 days, forced at the open boundary by CMEMS MED-MFC and at the surface by ERA5 blended with the coastal stations, with wave coupling and a heat flux model for temperature, establishes the circulation. It is validated against the BLUE buoy, moored 3.7 km off the Grand Harbour and recording waves, currents, temperature and salinity, against the HF radar surface currents over the Malta Channel, and against the tide gauges. All of these lie outside the basins or record sea level alone, so the current field inside the harbours is a model result and is reported as one.

**Renewal runs.** Renewal is computed over 60 simulated days, a duration comparable to the bottom renewal times of 32 and 61 days reported for Barcelona and Tarragona by Samper et al. (2022), with tracers released by inlet and with Lagrangian particles through the OpenDrift pipeline. Residence time is reported as a range. The partition between mechanisms is obtained from runs in which one forcing is withheld at a time. The same current and wind fields drive the oil drift scenarios for the industrial area of the Grand Harbour.

**Geometry experiments.** Each modification of Section 4.2 is a variant of the mesh and of the land mask on which the renewal run is repeated, together with the sweep where the mouth is concerned, and the difference from the present geometry is the result. Each variant adds a renewal run of 60 days and, for the sweep, some 8 hours of computation.

## 6. Climate change as a bounded discussion element

The phenomenon is recurrent and damaging, and whether it intensifies under a changing climate follows as a question. It is treated here as a discussion element and as a route for subsequent work rather than as a work package, since its execution would exceed the period available.

### 6.1 Documented consequence

Flooding of low-lying coastal streets is reported at Marsaskala, Xemxija, Marsaxlokk and at Msida in the vicinity of the Workers' Memorial, in the absence of rain and under otherwise settled weather, which is the signature of a meteotsunami rather than of a storm surge. One reported event carried a vehicle into the sea. Storm Harry of January 2026 inundated waterfronts at Marsascala, Sliema and Birżebbuġa, and the group published an analysis of that event drawn from its own monitoring network.

Dated events are tabulated with their sources in Section 7 of [senglea_spectrum.md](senglea_spectrum.md). The University of Malta documents a milgħuba on 18 June 2019 from about 02:30 for some three hours, recorded at Portomaso and attributed to atmospheric gravity waves from the east. The press reports further events on 30 June 2022 at Marsaskala, St Julian's and Qawra, on 1 July 2023 at Marsaskala and Xemxija, where a car was carried into the sea, and on 13 June 2024 at Msida, where the group is quoted. The events of 2019 and 2023 began near 02:30 local time.

Msida lies at the head of Msida Creek, inside Marsamxett and therefore inside the model domain, and Sliema likewise lies within Marsamxett.

Damage inside the commercial port is documented separately, and it is of a different kind. Mazas and Farrugia record that the storms of February 2019 from the northeast and February 2023 from the east caused significant damage in the port, with significant wave heights reaching 7 to 8 m in the severe directional sectors. Those are wind-wave events and not meteotsunamis, and the two are kept distinct throughout. The harbour system thus has a documented wind-wave problem, for which a remedy is in design, and long-wave flooding documented at Msida, in a frequency band for which that remedy was not dimensioned.

### 6.2 Pathways of climate influence

Climate change acts on the hazard through the baseline on which an event is superimposed and through the distribution of the atmospheric forcing.

**The baseline.** Mean sea level rise raises the still water level. A meteotsunami of peak-to-trough amplitude A overtops a threshold of elevation Z when A/2 exceeds Z less the rise S, so the rise consumes freeboard directly.

| Threshold elevation | Amplitude required at present | With 0.5 m of rise | With 1.0 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.6 m | 1.2 m | 0.2 m | exceeded without an event |
| 1.0 m | 2.0 m | 1.0 m | exceeded without an event |
| 1.5 m | 3.0 m | 2.0 m | 1.0 m |

This pathway is quantifiable within the present study, since it requires only the superposition of a projected rise on the modelled response.

**The distribution of the forcing.** Meteotsunami generation requires particular synoptic conditions. Mid-tropospheric jet intensity, meandering and latitudinal range correlate most strongly with occurrence globally, frequently in conjunction with a strong thermal front in the lower troposphere and a surface low, and these are affected by a changing climate. Positive trends in temperature and maximum wind speed over the Adriatic between 1987 and 2017 have been reported as potentially already influencing meteotsunamigenic conditions at the synoptic scale. This pathway is not quantifiable within the present study, since it requires climate model output and a validated synoptic index.

### 6.3 Sensitivity of the resonant periods to sea level rise

A rise increases depth. In a harbour bounded by vertical quay walls it does so without altering the length of the basin, so the quarter-wave period shortens. In a natural bay with sloping margins the length increases also and partly offsets the effect. The Valletta harbours are of the first kind.

| System | Present period | +0.3 m | +0.5 m | +1.0 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grand Harbour | 17.2 min | -0.9% | -1.5% | -3.0% |
| Marsamxett | 18.4 min | -1.1% | -1.8% | -3.6% |
| Msida Creek, head | 7.0 min | -2.4% | -3.9% | -7.4% |
| Malta Plateau | 156.4 min | -0.1% | -0.2% | -0.3% |

The displacement of the harbour modes is between three and four per cent for a metre of rise, and on the plateau it is negligible. The periods of the resonance are therefore nearly invariant, and the hazard changes through the baseline rather than through the resonance. The shallow heads of the inlets are the exception, where the proportional change in depth is largest, and they are also where the flooding is reported.

### 6.4 Separation of the response surface from the forcing climate

The parametric sweep of Section 5 produces a response surface, that is the amplification of the harbour system as a function of the speed and direction of the atmospheric disturbance. That surface is a transfer function of the geometry and does not depend on the climate, whereas climate change acts on the input distribution, namely the frequency with which disturbances of a given speed and direction occur. The present study computes the transfer function once, and a subsequent study possessing projections of the synoptic forcing may convolve the two without repeating the hydrodynamic work. No climate scenario need be run through the model.

### 6.5 Scope

Within the present study: the response surface, the superposition of projected sea level rise, and a statement of the two pathways with the sensitivity figures above.

Outside it, as a continuation: evaluation of a synoptic index of the kind constructed by Šepić et al. (2016) against climate model output, which has so far been achieved only for the Balearic Islands, and its convolution with the response surface computed here.

**Additional reading.** *Climate projections of meteotsunami hazards*, Frontiers in Marine Science 10, 1167863 (2023), which reviews the three available approaches, namely synoptic indices, high-resolution numerical models, and ensemble or stochastic methods. **Šepić, J. et al. (2016)**, *Quantifying the probability of meteotsunami occurrence from synoptic atmospheric patterns*, Geophysical Research Letters.

## 7. Relation to the thesis argument

At the Stagnone the framework addresses wind-driven circulation over a vegetated bed in a lagoon less than a metre deep, and at Valletta it addresses the circulation and renewal of a deep engineered basin, with an atmospherically generated long wave as a component. The solver and the assembly are unchanged between the two sites while the physical regime differs, so the transferability argument of the thesis rests on the application of one pipeline to two regimes rather than on its repetition at a second lagoon. An extension to other bays of the Maltese Islands is of possible interest to the group, conditional on the outcome at Valletta, and would be a further instance of the same transfer.

## 8. Risks

**Validation inside the harbours.** The BLUE buoy lies 3.7 km off the entrance, the HF radar covers the channel, and the gauges record sea level, Senglea alone standing inside a basin. No measurement of current or of the temperature and salinity profile inside either harbour is in hand. The interior circulation and the residence times are then model estimates accompanied by a range, which is the form in which the harbour literature of Section 9.5 reports them, and no claim on the detailed interior current field is admissible without such a measurement. Releases of surface drifters inside the harbours were raised by the group as a possibility. Vessel traffic limits them to short periods or to hours of low traffic, and tracks of a few hours would still test the surface currents and the Lagrangian pipeline at the places where the model is otherwise unconstrained.

**Geometry of the planned structures.** The paper of Mazas and Farrugia carries a plan at figure scale and the press reports approximate lengths, neither of which is sufficient to modify a bathymetry. Drawings of the planned works and of the Msida pier are to be sought through the group. Without them Section 4.2 is run as a sensitivity over a range of restrictions, which does not require the exact geometry and whose result, a response curve, transfers to other basins.

**Reading of the counterfactual as a comment on a public project.** A result about the geometry of a harbour mouth may be read in that way, which is not the intention. The results are reported as a response over the modification, with any particular scheme identified at most as one point of it and named only where it is unavoidable. Any contact with Infrastructure Malta, should the work later warrant it, is a matter for the group and belongs to a stage after the results exist.

**Bathymetry at the heads of Msida Creek and Pietà Creek.** The 10 m grid has gaps there, in the area of the Msida pier and of the documented flooding, and no second source has been identified.

**Open boundary under the hindcast forcing.** The weakly reflective boundary was tested with an outgoing pulse and no incoming signal ([open_boundary_pulse_test.md](open_boundary_pulse_test.md)). The hindcast imposes the CMEMS signal through the same boundary, and the sweep carries a pressure disturbance across it, and neither case has been tested.

**Extent of the domain for the Proudman stage.** The growth of the wave under Proudman resonance increases with the distance travelled, and the domain of 86 by 66 km may truncate it. A subset of the scenarios is to be repeated on a larger domain.

**The Senglea sea level record.** The gauge, device 555 on the JRC TAD server, has been offline since 13 December 2024 and covers 43 per cent of its span, with two usable windows of some nine months in all, and none of the three milgħuba events since its installation is resolved in the public copy ([senglea_spectrum.md](senglea_spectrum.md)). The test of the response surface against events therefore depends on the archive of the group.

**Long-wave consequence inside the commercial port.** Section 6.1 records flooding at Msida together with events at Marsaskala, Xemxija, Marsaxlokk and Sliema, and significant damage within the port from the storms of February 2019 and February 2023. The port damage is wind-wave damage. The group regards the long-wave damage in the harbours as small, and any record of mooring failure or range action on berthed vessels would be held by Transport Malta and the terminals rather than in the public domain.

**Run count.** The sweep amounts to 96 barotropic runs and each geometry variant repeats it, while the three-dimensional runs grow by 60 simulated days for each variant. The number of variants is the quantity to be kept small.

### 9.3 Analogues

**Ciutadella, Menorca.** The canonical case, in which Proudman resonance on the shelf, shoaling and harbour resonance compound to an order of magnitude. The relevant work is that of Rabinovich and Monserrat on the *rissaga*, together with the modelling of the event of 15 June 2006.

**Meteotsunami ("Marrobbio") of 25–26 June 2014 on the Southwestern Coast of Sicily, Italy.** Pure and Applied Geophysics (2018). The same shelf system and the adjacent coast. The event is attributed to Proudman resonance on the western Sicilian shelf combined with local resonant conditions.

### 9.6 Long-wave modelling in the Sicilian Channel

**Laksono, F.A.T., Mishra, M., Fadlin and Kovács, J. (2026).** *Exploring the tsunami generation potential of major faults in the Sicilian Channel using 3D numerical modeling.* Ocean Modelling 199, 102625. Open access. Copy at `reference/`. Delft3D-FLOW applied to the generation, propagation and inundation of seismic tsunamis from the Gela Nappe Thrust, the Sciacca strike-slip fault and the Malta Graben Normal Fault, on a domain of approximately 175 by 165 km² over the same channel, with inundation carried into HEC-RAS on a 2 m grid.

The work is a methodological precedent rather than a source on the milgħuba. It documents a prior application of Delft3D to long-wave propagation in this basin. It states that the Riemann boundary condition is applied to prevent artificial reflection at the model boundaries, which is the boundary treatment adopted here. It also cites Proudman resonance in connection with the atmospheric pressure waves of the 2022 Hunga Tonga eruption, which places the mechanism of Section 1 within the regional tsunami literature.

The Malta Graben Normal Fault lies within the domain used here, and the seismic case shares the configuration, the bathymetry and the boundary treatment with the meteorological one. A seismic application is recorded as a possible continuation and is outside the present scope.

### 9.7 The site as an engineering object

**Mazas, F. and Farrugia, C.**, as listed in Section 2, together with the ARTELIA laboratory's own account of the wave disturbance study.

**Balzan, M.V. et al. (2022).** *Assessing nature-based solutions uptake in a Mediterranean climate: insights from the case-study of Malta.* Nature-Based Solutions 2, 100029. Copy at `reference/`. A governance and stakeholder analysis rather than a hydrodynamic source, drawing on 96 implemented cases and a SWOT analysis conducted under the Horizon 2020 ReNature project. It maps who is engaged with nature-based approaches in Malta and through which institutions, which bears on the framing of the WETWISE deliverable rather than on the physics. ## 10. Open matters

The group has indicated that sea level and one-minute pressure from its archive on the dates of the events, the history of the Senglea installation together with the Portomaso record, and data for validation are to be supplied on 12 October 2026. The following remain open.

1. Measurements of current, or of temperature and salinity profiles, inside either harbour, in any form, including port surveys and Water Framework Directive monitoring.
2. The feasibility of drifter releases inside the harbours, with the sites, the hours compatible with vessel traffic and the duration of each track, which the model can help to choose once the hindcast exists.
3. Drawings or layouts of the planned modifications of the breakwaters and of the pier at Msida.
4. Bathymetry at the heads of Msida Creek and Pietà Creek.
5. The levelling datum of the bathymetry against the zeros of the tide gauges.
6. The analysis of Storm Harry, January 2026, published by the group from its monitoring network, as a possible validation case.
7. Documented damage or operational disruption attributable to long waves in the Grand Harbour, as distinct from the wind-wave damage of February 2019 and February 2023.
8. Any assessment of the milgħuba under a changing climate for Malta, through a synoptic index or otherwise, for want of which Section 6 treats the matter as a discussion element.
