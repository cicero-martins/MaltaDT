*Prepared 24 September 2026 for discussion with the host group. Supersedes the question stated in Section 2 of [malta_valletta_model_plan.md](malta_valletta_model_plan.md), which is retained there as the point of departure.*

## 1. Classification of the phenomenon

The milgħuba is not a seiche in the general sense of water oscillating in a basin. It is an atmospherically generated long wave in the tsunami frequency band, and it belongs to a body of work on meteotsunamis that the project had not previously drawn upon. The Mediterranean instances carry local names, *rissaga* in the Balearics, *šćiga* in the Adriatic, and ***marrobbio* on the southwestern coast of Sicily**, the last of these occurring on the same shelf system and the adjacent coast.

That literature supplies a three-stage model of amplification which corresponds to the domain analysis recorded in [domain_and_discretisation.md](domain_and_discretisation.md).

**Stage one, Proudman resonance.** Energy transfers from the atmosphere to the sea most efficiently when an atmospheric pressure disturbance travels at the celerity of the long wave beneath it, U = √(gh). The condition selects the part of the shelf that generates the signal.

| Depth | √(gh) | |
|---|---|---|
| 60 m | 24.3 m/s | 87 km/h |
| 100 m | 31.3 m/s | 113 km/h |
| 150 m | 38.4 m/s | 138 km/h |
| 190 m | 43.2 m/s | 155 km/h |

The Malta Plateau, measured at 99 to 190 m on the north transect and 70 to 137 m on the east, is resonant with disturbances travelling between 94 and 155 km/h, which lies within the range of mesoscale convective systems and atmospheric gravity waves.

**Stage two, shoaling.** Green's law gives an amplification of (h₁/h₂)^¼ on the approach. From the plateau to the Grand Harbour at 16 m this is 1.75, and to Marsamxett at 13 m it is 1.84. At Ciutadella, where the inlet is 5 m deep, the equivalent figure is larger and the reported amplification correspondingly greater.

**Stage three, basin resonance.** The quarter-wave and Helmholtz estimates computed for the two harbours, 12 to 18 minutes.

At Ciutadella the literature reports approximately fourfold amplification by shoaling on the approach and a further threefold within the harbour, an order of magnitude in total over the wave generated by Proudman resonance. No equivalent decomposition has been published for Malta.

## 2. Prior work

The prior literature was surveyed after the plan was drafted. The outcome bears materially on what the study can claim as a contribution.

**Drago, A.F. (1999).** *A study on the sea level variations and the 'Milgħuba' phenomenon in the coastal waters of the Maltese Islands.* Doctoral dissertation, University of Southampton. Establishes the phenomenon from sea level gauges and offshore bottom pressure, attributes it to mesoscale atmospheric gravity waves, and analyses sea level variability across timescales by wavelet methods. The treatment concerns the northern coast and Mellieħa Bay.

**Drago, A.F. (2009).** *Sea level variability and the 'Milgħuba' seiche oscillations in the northern coast of Malta, Central Mediterranean.* Physics and Chemistry of the Earth 34, 948–970. The published treatment, drawing on 43 months of densely sampled sea level at Mellieħa Bay between 1993 and 1996. Establishes the band of 0.2 to 2 cph, the masking of the tidal signal, and the interpretation as shelf-scale resonance amplified within the embayments.

**Numerical modelling of coastal seiches in Malta.** Physics and Chemistry of the Earth, 2007, PII S1474706507000551. Authorship to be confirmed, most probably Drago and co-workers. This is the closest prior work. It simulates the generation and propagation of seiche oscillations in two adjacent wide-mouthed embayments of the northern coast, Mellieħa Bay and St Paul's Bay, using a two-dimensional depth-averaged version of the Princeton Ocean Model, and examines their response to **prescribed offshore long-period waves**. It reports that standing oscillations generated cross-shore on the shelf, that is leaky waves, force the extreme oscillations in the embayments more effectively than alongshore-propagating edge waves.

### Implications for the contribution

Four implications follow.

The **Valletta harbour system has not been modelled**. The published numerical work addresses the northern coast. Airy described the phenomenon in the Grand Harbour in 1878 and it appears not to have received numerical treatment since.

The prior work is **two-dimensional and depth-averaged**, so the vertical structure and any consequence for the renewal of harbour water remain untreated.

**The prior work already establishes the response of a Maltese embayment to an imposed offshore long wave.** Repeating that exercise at Valletta in three dimensions would be incremental. If the question is how a given basin responds to a long wave arriving from offshore, it has been posed and answered for this coast with a different basin, and the novelty would rest on the basin and the dimensionality alone, which is a narrow basis.

**The generation side remains unaddressed.** The prior work prescribes the offshore wave rather than producing it, so the coupling between the atmospheric disturbance and the shelf, and the partition of amplification between the shelf, the approach and the basin, are open for Malta.

## 3. Assessment of the question as originally formulated

The plan asked whether the coupled framework reproduces the observed seiche response of the two harbours. The formulation has three defects.

**The outcome is binary and asymmetrically informative.** An affirmative result confirms what is expected of a mature solver. A negative result is attributable to the configuration rather than to the physics, and is not publishable.

**It interrogates the instrument rather than the system.** The object of knowledge produced is the model itself. The economic significance of the site makes that a limited return on the opportunity.

**It has no constituency beyond the modelling community.** That sits poorly with a deliverable framed around a national pilot.

The criterion for reformulation follows from the defects. A model earns its place when it answers a question that observation alone cannot. Tide gauges record the behaviour of the sea. Attribution of that behaviour to mechanism, and evaluation of a configuration that does not exist, require a model.

## 4. Proposed questions

### 4.1 Principal, attribution of amplification

> Of the amplification between the open sea and the heads of the Valletta harbours, how is it partitioned between Proudman resonance over the Malta Plateau, shoaling on the approach, and resonance of the harbour basins themselves, and which atmospheric disturbance speeds and directions maximise that chain?

The question cannot be answered by observation, since no arrangement of gauges separates the three stages. It is continuous rather than binary. It is transferable, the method applying to any pairing of shelf and harbour, which serves the thesis argument. It addresses the generation side, which Section 2 identifies as unaddressed.

### 4.2 Accompanying, applied

> Which parts of the harbour system amplify most, and which geometric features control that?

The deliverable is a map of exposure by inlet and by quay, which is the form in which the result reaches the port authority and the WETWISE deliverable.

### 4.3 Consequential, and available as a fallback

> In a microtidal harbour without appreciable tide or freshwater input, what renews the water, and how much of the exchange driven by long waves constitutes genuine renewal rather than reversible oscillation?

Alternating currents displace water without necessarily exchanging it. The distinction is the classical return-flow problem and it is not resolvable without a model. The question carries independent value given the status of both harbours as heavily modified water bodies under the Water Framework Directive and the contamination documented by Romeo et al. (2015), and it exercises the three-dimensional and Lagrangian components of the framework which the prior two-dimensional work could not.

## 5. Experimental design

The design follows from the principal question and it addresses the largest standing risk in the project.

**A parametric sweep of the atmospheric disturbance replaces the hindcast of an individual event.** A moving pressure disturbance is imposed over the domain and its speed, direction, width and duration are swept. The model then maps the resonance structure of the shelf and harbour system, which is a property of the geometry rather than of any particular storm.

This removes the dependence on the trigger. ERA5 does not resolve the atmospheric gravity waves that initiate the milgħuba, and that limitation had constituted the principal threat to the project. Under a parametric sweep the reanalysis is not required to resolve them, since no event is being reproduced. The approach is also the one taken in the Ciutadella literature.

The role of the observations is inverted. They cease to be the forcing and become the test, addressing whether the events recorded by the PORTO network and the tide gauges occur under the atmospheric conditions the model identifies as resonant. That constitutes a prediction admitting refutation.

**Validation is relocated to the methods.** Whether the model reproduces observed sea level becomes a section of the methods rather than the question of the paper.

---

## 6. Consequences for the thesis argument

The transferability claim is strengthened. It ceases to be an assertion that the same pipeline was executed at a second site and becomes a demonstration that the pipeline answered a question in a physical regime other than the one for which it was constructed. At the Stagnone the framework addressed wind-driven circulation over a vegetated bed. At Valletta it addresses atmospherically generated long waves in an engineered basin. The solver and the assembly are unchanged and the physics is not, which constitutes a stronger test of reusability than a second lagoon would have provided.

---

## 7. Risks

**The hazard framing requires documented consequence.** Airy described the phenomenon in the Grand Harbour in 1878, so the historical record exists, but whether Malta has documented damage in the modern port, such as mooring failures, interruption of cargo operations or range action on berthed vessels, is not established. In its absence the hazard framing weakens and the question in Section 4.3 becomes the primary one.

**The prior work may extend beyond the published record.** Section 2 rests on a literature search rather than on consultation with its authors, who are members of the host group. The first action is to establish what exists, including unpublished material and student work.

**A Grand Harbour sea level record probably exists.** Drago is reported to have maintained installations at Mellieħa Bay, Portomaso, Marsaxlokk and the Grand Harbour. If a harbour record exists at adequate sampling, whether the Valletta basins carry energy at their own 12 to 18 minute mode can be established from the archive rather than assumed. This would also correct an error in the earlier analysis, which compared a computation for Valletta against a band observed at Mellieħa.

**The parametric sweep multiplies the run count.** A sweep over six speeds and eight directions amounts to forty-eight runs. Barotropic and uncoupled these are inexpensive, and the design should separate the barotropic mapping of resonance from the small number of three-dimensional runs required for the renewal question.

## 8. Reading list

Ordered by function.

### 8.1 Introductory treatments

**Rabinovich, A.B. (2009).** *Seiches and Harbor Oscillations.* Chapter 9 in Kim, Y.C. (ed.), *Handbook of Coastal and Ocean Engineering*, World Scientific, pp. 193–236. [Freely available](http://jadran.izor.hr/~vilibic/meteotsunami/Rabinovich_Handbook_2010_Chapter9.pdf). The principal introductory reference, covering basin modes, the Helmholtz or pumping mode, and range action on berthed vessels, written for coastal engineers.

**Coastal Wiki**, [Harbor resonance](https://www.coastalwiki.org/wiki/Harbor_resonance) and [Proudman resonance and meteo tsunamis](https://www.coastalwiki.org/wiki/Proudman_resonance_and_meteo_tsunamis). Short entries, suited to a first reading.

### 8.2 The field

**Vilibić, I. et al. (2025).** *Meteorological Tsunamis: From Local Hazard to Global Relevance.* Reviews of Geophysics. The current authoritative review.

**Monserrat, S., Vilibić, I. & Rabinovich, A.B. (2006).** *Meteotsunamis: atmospherically induced destructive ocean waves in the tsunami frequency band.* Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences 6, 1035–1051. The paper that consolidated the term and the mechanism.

**Vilibić, I. et al. (2020).** *The Mediterranean and Black Sea meteotsunamis: an overview.* Natural Hazards. Regional synthesis.

**Vilibić, I. et al. (2019).** *Twenty-Seven Years of Progress in the Science of Meteorological Tsunamis Following the 1992 Daytona Beach Event.* Pure and Applied Geophysics.

**Are meteotsunamis an underrated hazard?** Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A 373, 20140377 (2015). Relevant to the hazard framing.

### 8.3 Analogues

**Ciutadella, Menorca.** The canonical case, in which Proudman resonance on the shelf, shoaling and harbour resonance compound to an order of magnitude. The relevant work is that of Rabinovich and Monserrat on the *rissaga*, together with the modelling of the event of 15 June 2006.

**Meteotsunami ("Marrobbio") of 25–26 June 2014 on the Southwestern Coast of Sicily, Italy.** Pure and Applied Geophysics (2018). The same shelf system and the adjacent coast. The event is attributed to Proudman resonance on the western Sicilian shelf combined with local resonant conditions.

### 8.4 Malta

The three works listed in Section 2.

### 8.5 Renewal and residence time

**Samper, Y. et al. (2022).** *Water exchanges in Mediterranean microtidal harbours.* Water 14, 2012.

**Spatial variability analysis of renewal time in harbour environments using a Lagrangian model.** Journal of Marine Science and Engineering 13(2), 341 (2025).

## 9. Questions for the host group

1. **Does a sea level record exist for the Grand Harbour, and at what sampling interval?** Drago is reported to have maintained an installation there. A spectrum computed from it would establish whether the Valletta basins carry energy at their own 12 to 18 minute mode, which the present analysis can only infer.
2. **What is the full extent of prior work on the milgħuba**, including unpublished material, student dissertations and any treatment of the southern and eastern coasts.
3. **Is there documented damage or operational disruption** attributable to long waves in the Grand Harbour, held by Transport Malta, the port operators or the terminals.
4. **Would the group regard the attribution question of Section 4.1 as a contribution**, or does it lie closer to their own intentions than the published record indicates.
