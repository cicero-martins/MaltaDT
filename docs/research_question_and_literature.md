## 1. The phenomenon belongs to the meteotsunami literature

The milgħuba is not a seiche in the general sense of water sloshing in a basin. It is an atmospherically generated long wave in the tsunami frequency band, and it belongs to a mature and hazard-oriented body of work that the project had not been drawing on. The Mediterranean instances carry local names: *rissaga* in the Balearics, *šćiga* in the Adriatic, and ***marrobbio* on the southwestern coast of Sicily**, which is the same shelf system and the adjacent coast.

That literature supplies a three-stage model of amplification which maps directly onto the domain analysis in [domain_and_discretisation.md](domain_and_discretisation.md).

**Stage one, Proudman resonance.** Energy transfers from the atmosphere to the sea most efficiently when an atmospheric pressure disturbance travels at the celerity of the long wave beneath it, U = √(gh). The condition therefore selects which part of the shelf generates the signal.

| Depth | √(gh) | |
|---|---|---|
| 60 m | 24.3 m/s | 87 km/h |
| 100 m | 31.3 m/s | 113 km/h |
| 150 m | 38.4 m/s | 138 km/h |
| 190 m | 43.2 m/s | 155 km/h |

The Malta Plateau, measured at 99 to 190 m on the north transect and 70 to 137 m on the east, is resonant with disturbances travelling between 94 and 155 km/h, which is within the range of mesoscale convective systems and atmospheric gravity waves.

**Stage two, shoaling.** Green's law gives an amplification of (h₁/h₂)^¼ on the approach. From the plateau to the Grand Harbour at 16 m this is 1.75, and to Marsamxett at 13 m it is 1.84. At Ciutadella, where the inlet is 5 m deep, the equivalent figure is larger and the reported amplification correspondingly greater.

**Stage three, basin resonance.** The quarter-wave and Helmholtz estimates computed for the two harbours, 12 to 18 minutes.

At Ciutadella the literature reports roughly fourfold amplification by shoaling on the approach and a further threefold within the harbour, an order of magnitude in total over the wave generated by Proudman resonance. No equivalent decomposition exists for Malta.

## 2. What has already been done, and what has not

This section exists because the answer changes the contribution substantially and was not known when the plan was drafted.

**Drago, A.F. (1999).** *A study on the sea level variations and the 'Milgħuba' phenomenon in the coastal waters of the Maltese Islands.* Doctoral dissertation, University of Southampton. Establishes the phenomenon from sea level gauges and offshore bottom pressure, attributes it to mesoscale atmospheric gravity waves, and analyses sea level variability across timescales by wavelet methods. Concerned with the northern coast and Mellieħa Bay.

**Drago, A.F. (2009).** *Sea level variability and the 'Milgħuba' seiche oscillations in the northern coast of Malta, Central Mediterranean.* Physics and Chemistry of the Earth 34, 948–970. The published treatment, from 43 months of densely sampled sea level at Mellieħa Bay between 1993 and 1996. Establishes the band of 0.2 to 2 cph, the masking of the tidal signal, and the interpretation as shelf-scale resonance amplified within the embayments.

**Numerical modelling of coastal seiches in Malta.** Physics and Chemistry of the Earth, 2007, PII S1474706507000551. Authorship to be confirmed, most probably Drago and co-workers. **This is the closest prior work and it matters.** It simulates the generation and propagation of seiche oscillations in two adjacent wide-mouthed embayments of the northern coast, Mellieħa Bay and St Paul's Bay, using a two-dimensional depth-averaged version of the Princeton Ocean Model, and examines their response to **prescribed offshore long-period waves**. It reports that standing oscillations generated cross-shore on the shelf, that is leaky waves, force the extreme oscillations in the embayments more effectively than alongshore-propagating edge waves.

### Consequences for the contribution

Four things follow, and the third is uncomfortable.

The **Valletta harbour system has not been modelled**. The published numerical work addresses the northern coast. Airy described the phenomenon in the Grand Harbour in 1878 and it appears not to have been treated numerically since.

The prior work is **two-dimensional and depth-averaged**, so the vertical structure and any consequence for renewal of harbour water are untouched.

**The prior work already establishes the response of a Maltese embayment to an imposed offshore long wave.** Repeating that exercise at Valletta, in three dimensions, would be incremental. If the question is "how does this basin respond to a long wave arriving from offshore", it has been asked and answered for this coast, with a different basin. The novelty would rest on the basin and the dimensionality alone, which is thin.

**The generation side is where the ground is open.** The prior work prescribes the offshore wave rather than generating it, so the coupling between the atmospheric disturbance and the shelf, and the partition of amplification between the shelf, the approach and the basin, remain unaddressed for Malta.

## 3. Why the question as originally stated is weak

The plan asked whether the coupled framework reproduces the observed seiche response of the two harbours. That formulation has three defects and they should be named rather than worked around.

**The outcome is binary and asymmetrically informative.** An affirmative result confirms what was expected of a mature solver. A negative one is attributed to the configuration rather than to the physics, and is not publishable.

**It interrogates the instrument rather than the system.** The object of knowledge produced is the model. For a site of this economic weight that is a poor use of the opportunity.

**It has no stakeholder.** Nobody outside the modelling community has an interest in the answer, which sits badly with a deliverable framed around a national pilot.

The criterion for repair is straightforward. A model earns its place when it answers a question that observation alone cannot. Tide gauges record what the sea does. Only a model attributes that behaviour to mechanism, and only a model evaluates a configuration that does not exist.

## 4. Proposed questions

### Principal, attribution of amplification

> Of the amplification between the open sea and the heads of the Valletta harbours, how is it partitioned between Proudman resonance over the Malta Plateau, shoaling on the approach, and resonance of the harbour basins themselves, and which atmospheric disturbance speeds and directions maximise that chain?

The question is unanswerable by observation, since no arrangement of gauges separates the three stages. It is continuous rather than binary. It is transferable, the method applying to any shelf and harbour pair, which serves the thesis argument. And it addresses the generation side, which Section 2 identifies as the open ground.

### Accompanying, applied

> Which parts of the harbour system amplify most, and which geometric features control that?

The deliverable is a map of exposure by inlet and by quay. This is the form in which the result reaches the port authority and the WETWISE deliverable.

### Consequential, and a fallback should the hazard framing prove unsupported

> In a microtidal harbour without appreciable tide or freshwater input, what renews the water, and how much of the exchange driven by long waves is genuine renewal rather than reversible sloshing?

Alternating currents displace water without necessarily exchanging it. The distinction is the classical return-flow problem and it is not resolvable without a model. It has independent value given the status of both harbours as heavily modified water bodies under the Water Framework Directive and the contamination documented by Romeo et al. (2015), and it exercises the three-dimensional and Lagrangian parts of the framework that the prior two-dimensional work could not.

## 5. Experimental design

The design follows from the principal question and it resolves the largest standing risk in the project.

**A parametric sweep of the atmospheric disturbance replaces the hindcast of an event.** A moving pressure disturbance is imposed over the domain and its speed, direction, width and duration are swept. The model then maps the resonance structure of the shelf and harbour system, which is a property of the geometry rather than of any particular storm.

This dissolves the trigger problem. ERA5 does not resolve the atmospheric gravity waves that initiate the milgħuba, and that limitation had been the principal threat to the project. Under a parametric sweep the reanalysis is not required to resolve them, because no event is being reproduced. It is also the approach the Ciutadella literature takes.

The role of the observations inverts, to their advantage. They cease to be the forcing and become the **test**: do the events recorded by the PORTO network and the tide gauges occur under the atmospheric conditions the model identifies as resonant? That is a genuine prediction with a possible refutation.

**Validation returns to its proper place.** Whether the model reproduces observed sea level becomes a section of the methods, not the question of the paper.

## 6. Consequences for the thesis argument

The transferability claim is strengthened rather than weakened. It ceases to be an assertion that the same pipeline ran at a second site and becomes a demonstration that the pipeline answered a question in a different physical regime from the one it was constructed for. At the Stagnone the framework addressed wind-driven circulation over a vegetated bed. At Valletta it addresses atmospherically generated long waves in an engineered basin. Same solver, same assembly, different physics, which is a considerably stronger test of reusability than a second lagoon would have been.

## 7. Risks

**The hazard framing requires documented consequence.** Airy described the phenomenon in the Grand Harbour in 1878, so the historical record exists, but whether Malta has documented damage in the modern port, such as mooring failures, interruption of cargo operations or range action on berthed vessels, is not established. Without it the hazard framing weakens and the question in Section 4.3 becomes the primary one.

**The prior work may extend further than the published record indicates.** Section 2 rests on a literature search rather than on conversation with its authors, who are in the host group. The first action is to establish what exists, including unpublished material and student work.

**A Grand Harbour sea level record probably exists.** Drago is reported to have maintained installations at Mellieħa Bay, Portomaso, Marsaxlokk and the Grand Harbour. If a harbour record exists at adequate sampling, the question of whether the Valletta basins carry energy at their own 12 to 18 minute mode can be settled from the archive rather than assumed. This would also correct an error in the earlier analysis, which compared a computation for Valletta against a band observed at Mellieħa.

**The parametric sweep multiplies the run count.** A sweep over speed and direction at, say, six speeds and eight directions is forty-eight runs. Barotropic and uncoupled these are cheap, and the design should exploit that by separating the barotropic resonance mapping from the small number of three-dimensional runs required for the renewal question.

## 8. Reading list

Ordered by what each is for.

### The concept, for a reader who is not a physical oceanographer

**Rabinovich, A.B. (2009).** *Seiches and Harbor Oscillations.* Chapter 9 in Kim, Y.C. (ed.), *Handbook of Coastal and Ocean Engineering*, World Scientific, pp. 193–236. [Freely available](http://jadran.izor.hr/~vilibic/meteotsunami/Rabinovich_Handbook_2010_Chapter9.pdf). The reference to read first. Basin modes, the Helmholtz or pumping mode, and range action on berthed vessels, written for coastal engineers.

**Coastal Wiki**, [Harbor resonance](https://www.coastalwiki.org/wiki/Harbor_resonance) and [Proudman resonance and meteo tsunamis](https://www.coastalwiki.org/wiki/Proudman_resonance_and_meteo_tsunamis). Short entries, useful before the papers.

### The field

**Vilibić, I. et al. (2025).** *Meteorological Tsunamis: From Local Hazard to Global Relevance.* Reviews of Geophysics. The current authoritative review.

**Monserrat, S., Vilibić, I. & Rabinovich, A.B. (2006).** *Meteotsunamis: atmospherically induced destructive ocean waves in the tsunami frequency band.* Natural Hazards and Earth System Sciences 6, 1035–1051. The paper that consolidated the term and the mechanism.

**Vilibić, I. et al. (2020).** *The Mediterranean and Black Sea meteotsunamis: an overview.* Natural Hazards. Regional synthesis.

**Vilibić, I. et al. (2019).** *Twenty-Seven Years of Progress in the Science of Meteorological Tsunamis Following the 1992 Daytona Beach Event.* Pure and Applied Geophysics.

**Are meteotsunamis an underrated hazard?** Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A 373, 20140377 (2015). For the hazard framing.

### The analogues

**Ciutadella, Menorca.** The canonical case, where Proudman resonance on the shelf, shoaling and harbour resonance compound to an order of magnitude. Look to Rabinovich and Monserrat on the *rissaga*, and to the modelling of the event of 15 June 2006.

**Meteotsunami ("Marrobbio") of 25–26 June 2014 on the Southwestern Coast of Sicily, Italy.** Pure and Applied Geophysics (2018). The same shelf system and the adjacent coast. Attributes the event to Proudman resonance on the western Sicilian shelf combined with local resonant conditions.

### Malta

The three works in Section 2.

### Renewal and residence time

**Samper, Y. et al. (2022).** *Water exchanges in Mediterranean microtidal harbours.* Water 14, 2012.

**Spatial variability analysis of renewal time in harbour environments using a Lagrangian model.** Journal of Marine Science and Engineering 13(2), 341 (2025).
