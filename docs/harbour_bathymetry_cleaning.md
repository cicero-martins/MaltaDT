## 1. Returns from pontoons and vessels

The merged 10 m field of 4036_MEPA ([mepa_4036_dataset.md](mepa_4036_dataset.md)) holds, along the marina pontoons of Ta' Xbiex, Msida, Pietà and the creeks of the Grand Harbour, rows of cells without a value and rows of values near or above zero in water several metres deep, aligned with the piers. At the head of the Grand Harbour, at Marsa, it holds contiguous patches of levels up to +14 m. These are read as the LiDAR survey recording the decks of floating pontoons and the hulls of moored vessels, or no usable return, in place of the seabed. Aerial imagery shows a marina of pontoons for small craft at the head of Msida Creek and, at Marsa, a shipyard basin with large vessels and barges moored alongside the quays, both in water.

Within a box enclosing both harbours, 14.480 to 14.540 E and 35.870 to 35.915 N, a water cell of the coastline polygon is removed when

- it carries no value,
- its level is at or above +0.3 m, at any distance from the coastline, since a bed above mean sea level inside the water polygon is a deck, a hull or a quay, the emerged part of a beach lying on the land side of the coastline,
- its level is at or above −0.5 m more than 20 m from the coastline, or
- it stands more than 2.5 m above the median of the valid water cells in a window of 7 by 7 cells, again more than 20 m from the coastline.

The removed set is widened by one cell, since the edge of a deck or hull yields mixed returns. The removed cells are filled by inverse-distance weighting from the remaining water cells, with the FillNodata algorithm of GDAL, a search in four directions within 300 m and no smoothing, land cells being excluded as sources. The limits are working values. The distance from the coastline in the last two criteria protects the shallow fringe along the quays, which a window spanning the fringe and the channel would otherwise read as a spike, and the limit of 2.5 m spares the break of slope at the edge of the dredged channels, which a limit of 1.5 m removed. The criterion at +0.3 m is the one that reaches the vessels at Marsa, which lie alongside the quays and are wider than the window of the spike test.

| Criterion | Cells | Share of harbour water |
|---|---|---|
| No value | 904 | 1.2 % |
| At or above +0.3 m, or at or above −0.5 m beyond 20 m from the coast | 437 | 0.6 % |
| More than 2.5 m above the local median | 561 | 0.8 % |
| Edge, one cell | 4 212 | 5.6 % |
| All | 6 114 | 8.2 % |

The level of the removed cells that carried a value changes by a median of −1.19 m, between −8.25 and +1.47 m for 90 per cent of them. Harbour water cells at or above zero fall from 582 to 60.

The interpolated bed under the vessels at Marsa follows the surrounding basin, some 2 to 4 m. A shipyard basin holding vessels of this size is likely to be deeper, so the bed there is a lower bound on the depth until a survey of the basin is in hand.

## 2. Passage under the Manoel Island bridge

The supplied coastline ([coastline_dataset.md](coastline_dataset.md)) draws the Manoel Island bridge as land, separating the arm of Gżira from the head of Lazzaretto Creek by 11.6 m, where aerial imagery shows water on both sides of the bridge deck. A rectangle 20 m wide is removed from the land polygon along the line joining the nearest points of the two water bodies and extended 15 m into each. The width of the span is a working value, to be replaced by a measurement. The survey records the bridge deck there, so the bed in the passage is filled from the two arms and is held at the minimum depth of Section 3, 0.5 m, which leaves the exchange through the passage to be confirmed against a measured depth.

## 3. Sampling of the mesh

The node levels of version 02 are bilinear samples of the merged field, and a sample is without value as soon as one of the four surrounding cells is a gap or lies outside the field. Such a node took the EMODnet level, whose cells of some 115 m inside the harbours are land. Inside the harbour box 710 nodes received EMODnet levels, 662 of them at or above zero with a median of +5.8 m and up to +15.4 m. Together with the returns from pontoons and vessels this left 717 harbour faces at or above zero, the marina basin at the head of Msida Creek and the shipyard basin at Marsa among them.

Version 03 samples a second field in which, in addition, land cells within 30 m of water take the level of the nearest water cell. A node on the coastline then samples the bed beside the quay wall rather than the level of the quay, and a node near the shore no longer meets a gap. Cells farther from water keep no value, and outside the MEPA coverage the node falls back to EMODnet as before. Inside the harbours, which are lined by quays and hold no intertidal flats, the nodes are held at a depth of at least 0.5 m, a working value.

## 4. Consequences for the results to date

The sweep of the long wave ([longwave_sweep.md](longwave_sweep.md)) and the interactive maps were computed on outer01, with the marina basin at the head of Msida Creek and the shipyard basin at Marsa dry, Lazzaretto Creek closed at its head, and the station at the head of Msida Creek placed 261 m from the intended point. The responses at Marsa and at the heads of Msida and Pietà Creeks are expected to change on outer03, and the sweep is to be repeated before those values are used. The response at Senglea and off the harbours, where the bed is unchanged, is not expected to change materially.

## 5. Matters open

1. Confirmation of the limits of Section 1 against the pontoons mapped in imagery or in OpenStreetMap.
2. The depth of the shipyard basin at Marsa beneath the moored vessels.
3. The span and depth of the passage under the Manoel Island bridge, and whether other bridges in the harbours close water in the supplied coastline.
4. The removed cells mark the footprint of pontoons and moored vessels at the time of the survey, and serve as a first map of the floating structures, whose effect on the surface flow can be represented in D-Flow FM through a forced ice cover of prescribed thickness and area fraction (User Manual, Sections 12.14 and 16). The fixed piers on piles can be represented by the rigid vegetation model of cylinders (Section 13.4).
